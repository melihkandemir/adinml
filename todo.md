**Remarks on the proofs that were left incomplete (all others are now proved in full):**

1. *Theorem 5.4 (Fundamental Theorem of Statistical Learning).* The equivalence of the six statements is proved completely, as a closed implication cycle. The **rates** are not fully tight:
   - The **upper** bound is proved up to a residual $\log(1/\epsilon)$ factor, i.e. $m_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathrm{UC}} = O\big((d\log(1/\epsilon)+\log(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2\big)$ instead of $O\big((d+\log(1/\delta))/\epsilon^2\big)$. Removing that factor requires the **chaining / Dudley entropy integral** argument, which is stated but not developed in Chapter 5a.
   - The **lower** bound is proved only in its $\Omega(d)$ form (via No Free Lunch on a shattered set). Sharpening it to $\Omega(d/\epsilon)$ (realizable) and $\Omega(d/\epsilon^2)$ (agnostic) needs a refinement of the No Free Lunch construction in which the labels on the shattered set are $\epsilon$-biased coins rather than deterministic, so the learner must estimate $d$ separate biases. That is a genuinely harder argument (Shalev-Shwartz Ch. 28) and is omitted.
   - The realizable-case $O(1/\epsilon)$ rate additionally invokes the multiplicative (relative-deviation) Chernoff bound, which is cited but not proved in Chapter 4.
2. *Theorem 9.1 (Mercer), converse direction.* Now **resolved** by the new RKHS section: Theorem 9.2 (Moore-Aronszajn) constructs the feature map $\phi(x) := k(x,\cdot)$ and verifies well-definedness, bilinearity, symmetry, positive semi-definiteness, the reproducing property and definiteness in full. The single step still omitted is the analytic **completion** of the pre-Hilbert space $\mathcal{V}$ into a Hilbert space (and the uniqueness claim), which is standard functional analysis.
3. *Talagrand's contraction lemma (Lemma 5a.2)* is proved in the **unsigned** form used throughout the notes. The signed variant, which carries an extra factor of $2$ unless $\phi_i(0)=0$, is stated but not proved; the place where the argument breaks is pointed out explicitly.
